## Round 13 rules

- **R90 Block as a cluster hint.** A footprint block is a hint at a cluster and never a unit code. analysis.blockClusters in config/project.json maps the name of a block, without the xref prefix the CAD reader puts in front (the text after the last dollar sign), to a cluster code of the profile: Duplex_DP2 D02, Duplex_DP4 D04, Townhouse_TH1 Q01 and Townhouse_TH3 Q02. A map that is not a map of names to text, or that names a code which is not a cluster of the profile, stops the analysis with a ProfileError. Every unit block that stands for 2 or more plots (R69) and has a listed name is a hint with block, hint (the cluster code), plots (by number), cluster, codeAgrees and a status. size means the block stands for another number of plots than the cluster has units. no schedule means no plot of it has an Excel unit and partly schedule that some have. equal means its plots are exactly the plots of one cluster of this run, and cluster is that cluster code. differs means they are not, and cluster lists the codes of the clusters its plots are in. For an equal hint codeAgrees says whether the cluster code and the hint code are the same after cluster_aliases. A hint never gives a plot a unit code and never changes a cluster. The hints with status differs or size are the conflicts of analysis.json and are never settled. The summary under hints holds blocks, groups (the distinct sets of plots, two blocks can stand for the same plots), byStatus, byHint, equalCluster, equalGroups, codeAgrees, codeDiffers, clustersOfSeveralPlots and clustersWithHintGroup. The issues of the block step carry the rule R90: a plot with two or more deciding blocks, a block that stands for more plots than cluster_block_max of the profile (it names the block and its plots), and a block drawing with no unit block or with unit blocks that have no outline of three points.
- **R91 Road level lookup.** python/road_lookup.py asks the LandXML surfaces one after the other and the first that covers the point answers. level_at(easting, northing) returns the level and the surface name, or none and none. The ground surface is never asked. The order comes from config/road_surfaces.json and no code writes that file. When every surface that is not ground has an order key the keys decide, they must be different numbers, and a mix of surfaces with and without one, a key that is not a number or an order on a ground surface is refused with a message. When none has a key the road surface is first and the design surfaces follow in the order of the file, and orderSource says file order, road first. Bader has set no order in the file, so the file order is used and the reports say so. ramp_levels.load_config carries order and orderSource. A role other than ground, design or road is refused with a message, so the ground can never answer through a typo. Two surfaces with the role road are refused.
- **R92 Ramp test with the lookup.** The ramp test of R44 to R47 also runs every corner through the lookup of R91. road_levels_summary.json holds under lookup the order, orderSource, the corners answered by each surface, the corners no surface answered, the ramps by number of covered corners, the ramps no corner of which the first surface covers, how many of those a later surface covers and by which surface. Each corner of ramp_road_levels.json also holds lookupLevel and lookupSurface. The earlier tables stay as they were.
- **R93 Entry measurement.** python/entry_proof.py measure compares El @ Entry of the schedule with each candidate point of the ramp of the same plot on the past job, which is the answer key named by pastJob in config/project.json. The candidates are the highest and lowest corner, the mean of the corners, the corner nearest and farthest from the link origin, the highest and lowest of the four edge midpoints, the midpoint nearest and farthest from the link origin, the midpoint and both ends of the higher long edge, the midpoint of the lower long edge and the top elevation of the floor. It also compares the road levels from the lookup at the covered ramp corners (mean and highest), at the curb point of the front edge of the plot, at the midpoint of the front edge and half way between them. A ramp is joined to its plot face by the face that holds its centre. For each candidate it gives the number of plots, the median, the 5th and 95th percentile, the largest absolute difference and the plots within 5 mm, 10 mm and 30 mm, as they are and after the median is taken off. entry_measure.json goes outside git and holds counts and distances only.
- **R94 Entry rule.** Measured from past work. The entry point of a ramp is the midpoint of its higher long edge, the long edges being those of R59 and the higher one the one with the larger mean level. The entry level is the mean level of its two corners. A ramp whose long edges are level with each other has no higher edge, the first is taken and flat says so. A plot with no ramp gets the road level at its curb point plus a measured offset. The front edge of a plot is the edge of its plot face, not shorter than 1 m, whose midpoint is nearest to a road edge line of R71. tied says another edge is within 1 m of the nearest, which is a corner plot. The curb point is the point of that road edge line nearest to the midpoint of the front edge. The level is the lookup of R91 at the curb point plus the offset, with the surface that answered. A curb point no surface covers gives no level, which is listed and never filled in. The offset is the median of El @ Entry minus the road level at the curb point over the plots of the past job and lives in config/prototype_offsets.json under entry (R100), with the note measured from past work. python/entry_levels.py holds the rule and no value of it.
- **R95 Entry proof.** python/entry_proof.py proof works out the entry level of every plot of the schedule workbooks of the project settings by R94 and compares it with El @ Entry of the sheet. The baseplan face of a sheet plot is the one with the label that is its official number minus the shift of R28, and a label held by no face or by two is listed. For each workbook it gives the plots, those with and without a road level, the plots within 5 mm, 10 mm and 30 mm (cumulative) and worse, the spread after the median, the surface that answered, the plots with a tied front edge, the 15 worst plots and the plots with no road level. entry_proof.json also holds the computed entry level of each plot by baseplan label, kept to 4 decimals, and the curb points. The past job is in the offset, so a workbook with no ramp in the past job is the test that was not part of the fit. The report goes outside git.
- **R96 Computed entry levels through the level rule.** python/levels_proof.py with --analysis and --entries feeds levels.compute (R67) with the clusters of analysis.json and the computed entry levels of R95, and compares the FFL and the steps with the sheet. The same clusters are also run with the sheet entry levels. A plot that misses only with the computed entry level has the cause entry level. A plot that misses in both runs takes the cause of the run with the sheet entry (wrong cluster, base height, rounding, no unit code or other), so the causes add up to the misses. A plot with no computed entry level has no FFL and misses with the cause entry level when the sheet entry would have matched. The summary holds computedEntry with the plots, those with a computed entry level, the bands of computed minus sheet entry level, the FFL and steps matches, both and the causes.
- **R97 Block offset.** python/block_offsets.py measures, for each plot of the past job that has a villa link and one deciding block (R69), the offset from the insert point of the block to the origin of the link in the axes of the block. The axes of the block are the rotation and the scale of the insert, so a block flipped in X has its local X axis running the other way. The offset is the world difference turned into those axes and divided by the scale. The full transform of the link against the block is the matrix of the link axes in the axes of the block. It is given as the rotation of its X axis from 0 up to 360 and whether its determinant is negative, which is mirrored, and as scaled when it is not a rotation or a reflection. It is never read from the two flags alone, a flipped link turned 180 degrees is another relation than a plain one. A block that stands for 1, 2 or 4 plots counts, any other number, a scale of 0, a plot with no face, no block or two blocks, and a link without both axes are left out and counted. The position of a unit inside its block, 1 up, is counted along the local X axis of the block from the centroids of the plot faces it stands for, plots in one column of a block of four are told apart by the local Y axis and then the face index. A scale that is not two numbers is left out like a scale of 0. A group is a block name, without the xref prefix, and a position.
- **R98 Fit and test per group.** Each group of R97 is split in half. The plots are put in order of plot number, shuffled with random.Random(13) and cut, the first half is the larger when the count is odd. One offset (the median of both axes), one rotation and one mirror flag (the most common pair, a tie to the smaller) are fitted on the first half and tested on the second. The error of a plot is the distance in the world between the link origin the fit predicts and the measured one, and the groups report the plots within 1 mm, within 10 mm and worse for both halves, and how many test plots have the fitted rotation within 0.1 degrees and the same mirror flag. A group with fewer than 4 plots is reported as tooFew and not tested. Each group also lists its variants, the relations it holds with the plots and median offset of each, because a mirrored unit has its origin on the other side of its plot. The seed is in the report and in the offsets file.
- **R99 Entrance point per block.** The entry point of R94 of the ramp of each plot is turned into the axes of the block the same way as the link origin and fitted and tested per group with the same split. Its error bands are within 10 mm, within 100 mm and worse. The entrance of a group is the median over all its plots with a ramp. A plot with no ramp has none and a group with none has no entrance test.
- **R100 Measured offsets file.** config/prototype_offsets.json holds the values measured on the past job. The section entry holds roadOffsetM of R94. The section groups holds one entry for each group of R97 with block, position, plots, tooFew, offset, relative (rotationDeg, mirrored, plotsWithIt and differentRelations), entrance, variants and, for a group that was tested, splitSeed, fitError, testError, relationRightOnTest and entranceTestError. The offset, the relation and the entrance are the medians over all plots of the group and the errors are those of the split of R98. The note says the values are measured from the IMC past job and are not a rule of the client. python/prototype_offsets.py reads the file and writes one section at a time, a missing file, bad JSON or a missing section is a ProfileError, and no Python file holds a value of it. The section plotFrame holds the frame of R121 to R123: front, linkAxes, and per typology anchor, offset and drawnSide, read by prototype_offsets.plot_frame, which refuses an anchor that is not start or end, an offset that is not two numbers or a drawnSide that is not left, right or null. The values are written by entry_proof.py measure with --write-offsets, by block_offsets.py with --write-offsets and by cad_links.py with --write-offsets, never by hand. Lengths are in metres and angles in degrees.
- **R101 Second methods.** python/round13_check.py checks R91 and R97 another way on a random sample drawn with a seed that the report holds. road draws points from the curb points of entry_proof.json and finds the level with a plain search over every triangle of every surface in the order, using no grid, and it must give the same surface and the same level within a micrometre. offsets draws plots from block_offsets.json and works out the offset, the rotation and the mirror flag again from the raw prototypes.json and blocks.json with plain trigonometry and no matrix helper, and it must give the same offset within 0.8 mm (the report rounds each axis to the millimetre) and the same rotation within 0.05 degrees. Each reports the points or plots drawn, how many agree and the ones that do not.

## Round 15b rules

- **R111 Yard rule.** A yard floor belongs to the plot whose baseplan plot face holds the largest share of its area (Bader, 24 Oct). python/yard_rule.py reads the plot_shape faces of plots.json of the CAD reader run on the baseplan, named by pastJob.baseplanPlots in config/project.json. The official number of a face is its label plus the shift of the neighbourhood (R28), and a neighbourhood with no shift stops the run with a ProfileError. A label that is not a whole number is left out and counted, and two faces with one label are joined. The share of a face is the area of the yard on it over the area of the yard. Shares within floors.yardShareTie (0.01) of the best are a tie, listed with the candidates and never guessed, and a yard on no face is none. A best share under floors.yardMinShare (0.5) is lowShare and the yard goes to no plot, so a yard that lies mostly on a face with no usable label never lands on the neighbour that holds a sliver of it. A plot with no yard says why: a tie that names it, a lowShare yard that would have gone to it, no face with its label, or no yard with its largest share on it. Two yards on one plot are listed and neither is used. The touch rule of R58 is not used for the plan, it gave 2 to 6 candidates on every plot. summary.yardRule holds the yards by status and the yards that go to no plot of the plan, with their best candidates.
- **R112 Floors in the plan.** Each plot of plan.json holds under floors a generic, a yard and a ramp floor when the past job gives them, each with type, level, levelElevation (shared elevation, from the past floor), heightOffset, outline, plotValue and, for a flat floor, z. The outline holds loops, each with points in shared plan coordinates and arcs (edge, center, radius, ccw), where an arc replaces the straight edge from point edge to the next one. The first loop is the sketch outline the reader gives with its arcs (R34), and every other loop, a second piece or a hole, comes from the top face outline of R52, with straight edges. A top face loop is the sketch loop again and is left out when the area the two do not share (their symmetric difference) is no more than 1 percent of the sketch loop, with the arcs of the sketch cut into chords that stand off them by 0.1 mm at most. An arc whose ends are not on its circle within 1 mm, or that names no edge, keeps the floor out. z is the FFL for the Generic floor and the FFL minus floors.yardBelowFflM (0.100 m) for the yard, and heightOffset is z minus levelElevation. A ramp holds its four corners with their own z and heightOffset is the lowest corner minus levelElevation, and its corners must sit on its one outline loop within 2 mm. plotValue is unit-plot, for example VD2-2441, on all three floors, and a past Plot value that differs is a conflict. A floor whose level is not floors.levels of its kind is listed and not written. The floor kind of a past floor is its type name in floors.types, or in floors.keyTypes of the answer key the past model is (R321), so no type or level name sits in a Python or C# file. The plan holds floorSettings with the types, the levels and the plot parameter, which is what the placer reads. A past top that differs from z by more than 5 mm is a conflict and z is kept.
